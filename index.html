Research

My research sits at the intersection of multimodal AI, 3D computer vision, and computer graphics. I am interested in fine-grained 3D modelling of human motion, of both the full body and the hands, where a few degrees at a joint or a fraction of a beat change the meaning; in generative models for expressive, audio-driven motion and avatar synthesis; in reference-based evaluation of motion that separates spatial error from timing error; and in multimodal, low-resource learning that aligns audio with motion and recovers structure from very little labelled data. Earlier, I worked on video understanding, including emotion recognition and summarization from surveillance footage, and on improving contextual speech recognition.

Publications & Preprints

  1. Footwork2Framework: A Large-Scale Dataset and Generative Framework for Expressive, Fine-Grained Audio-Driven 3D Human Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan*, Lipisha Nitin Chaudhary*, Tanvi Ranga, Ifeoma Nwogu, Rohini Srihari

    under review WACV 2027

    The largest 3D dance-motion dataset with synchronized full-body and hand meshes (1,910 clips, about 2.5× AIST++), plus a VQ-VAE model that generates expressive motion from audio. An earlier version appeared at the Sustainable Databases in the Humanities workshop, 2025.

    project page scholar

  2. Transition
    Correctness

    Transition Correctness: A Reference-Based Measure for Path and Timing Fidelity in Fine-Grained Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan, Lipisha Nitin Chaudhary, Ifeoma Nwogu, Rohini Srihari

    under review ICLR 2027

    A way to score how well a generated motion transitions between poses, checking anatomical plausibility, the path through pose space, and timing, and telling spatial errors apart from pacing errors. Works from two or three expert clips, no labelled mistakes needed.

    paper soon

  3. Investigating Fine-Grained Structure in Discrete 3D Human Motion Representations

    Ankitha Sudarshan, Lipisha Nitin Chaudhary, Ifeoma Nwogu, Rohini Srihari

    under review 3DV 2027

    A VQ-VAE trained on body and hand motion with no labels still learns tokens that separate the dance moves. Body tokens carry the movement category; hand tokens carry who is dancing.

    paper soon

  4. Low-Resource Rhythm Learning of South Asian Beat Structures: Machine Learning Approaches to Nattuvangam

    Ankitha Sudarshan, Atharva Vikas Jadhav, Rohini Srihari

    AAAI 2026 Workshop on Emerging AI Technologies for Music (PMLR)  ·  abstract at the NeurIPS 2025 Women in ML Workshop

    A first labelled dataset of South Asian dance rhythm (nattuvangam), and a model that recognises the rhythmic cycle at 76.2% accuracy from very little data, transferring zero-shot to rare cycles.

    poster scholar

  5. Improved Contextual Recognition in Automatic Speech Recognition Systems by Semantic Lattice Rescoring

    Ankitha Sudarshan, Vinay Samuel, Parth Patwa, Ibtihel Amara, Aman Chadha

    arXiv:2310.09680, 2023  ·  Purdue University

    Re-ranking the speech recogniser's word lattice with a transformer that understands meaning, which cuts word error rate by 14.88% on LibriSpeech. I framed the problem and led a five-person team.

    arXiv
