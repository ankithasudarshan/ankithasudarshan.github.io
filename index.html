Research

I work on fine-grained human motion, at the intersection of multimodal AI and 3D computer vision. I build datasets, generative models, and evaluation methods for expressive full-body and hand motion, where a few degrees at a joint or a fraction of a beat change the meaning. Along the way I have released the largest 3D dataset of its kind (Footwork2Framework), a reference-based measure for motion transitions (Transition Correctness), and hold a granted-track India patent from earlier work in video understanding.

Before the PhD I spent about two years as a Data Scientist at KPMG and Graphene Health Tech, shipping production NLP and conversational-AI systems.

Datasets & generation

Synchronized full-body and hand mesh motion at scale, and generative models (VQ-VAE, audio-conditioned) that keep the detail. See Footwork2Framework and discrete motion representations.

Motion evaluation

Reference-based measures that separate where a motion goes wrong in space from when it goes wrong in time, which DTW-style metrics conflate. See Transition Correctness.

Multimodal & low-resource

Aligning audio and motion, and learning structure from little labelled data, from South Asian rhythm to ASR lattice rescoring.

Publications & Preprints

Bold indicates my name. under review marks work not yet accepted. A full list is on my Google Scholar profile.

  1. Footwork2Framework: A Large-Scale Dataset and Generative Framework for Expressive, Fine-Grained Audio-Driven 3D Human Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan*, Lipisha Nitin Chaudhary*, Tanvi Ranga, Ifeoma Nwogu, Rohini Srihari

    under review WACV 2027

    The first 3D motion dataset with synchronized full-body and hand mesh annotations (1,910 clips, roughly 47,750 s, about 2.5× the size of AIST++), plus a VQ-VAE generation framework conditioned on motion and audio, with bidirectional audio-motion alignment and appearance retargeting for avatar synthesis. An earlier version appeared at the Sustainable Databases in the Humanities workshop, 2025.

    project page scholar

  2. Transition
    Correctness

    Transition Correctness: A Reference-Based Measure for Path and Timing Fidelity in Fine-Grained Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    under review ICLR 2027

    A three-stage, reference-based measure that scores motion transitions in a unified joint-angle space: an anatomical-feasibility gate, path correctness against an expert reference corridor, and timing correctness that separates spatial error from pacing error. Needs only two or three expert demonstrations and no error-labelled training data; validated on dance, surgical kinematics, and sign language.

    paper soon

  3. Investigating Fine-Grained Structure in Discrete 3D Human Motion Representations

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    under review 3DV 2027

    Trains a VQ-VAE on 3D body and articulated-hand motion with no category or performer labels, then probes what the codebook learns. Body tokens recover movement category well above a majority baseline; hand-only tokens carry more performer-specific information, evidence that reconstruction-only objectives implicitly encode category structure.

    paper soon

  4. Rhythm
    Learning

    Low-Resource Rhythm Learning of South Asian Beat Structures: Machine Learning Approaches to Nattuvangam

    Ankitha Sudarshan, A. V. Jadhav, Rohini Srihari

    AAAI 2026 Workshop on Emerging AI Technologies for Music (PMLR)  ·  abstract at the NeurIPS 2025 Women in ML Workshop

    The first annotated structured-beat dataset for South Asian music (31,560 s, 7 classes). A hybrid MFCC + CLAP model reaches 76.2% accuracy (F1 0.73) over a 69.8% baseline, with zero-shot transfer to rare classes under 30:1 class imbalance.

    scholar

  5. Improved Contextual Recognition in Automatic Speech Recognition Systems by Semantic Lattice Rescoring

    Ankitha Sudarshan, Vinay Samuel, Parth Patwa, Ibtihel Amara, Aman Chadha

    arXiv, 2023  ·  Purdue University

    A transformer-based rescoring step over the ASR word lattice, on top of an HMM-GMM + DNN pipeline. Cuts word error rate by 14.88% after rescoring and by 1.36% against comparable systems on LibriSpeech. I framed the problem and led a five-person international team.

    arXiv
