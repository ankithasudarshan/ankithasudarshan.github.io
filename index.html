Research

I want machines to understand and generate human motion at the level of detail a trained eye sees — where a few degrees at a joint or a fraction of a beat change the meaning. My work runs across three connected threads:

Fine-grained 3D motion datasets & generation

Capturing synchronized full-body and hand mesh motion at scale, and training generative models (VQ-VAE, audio-conditioned) that respect that detail — e.g. Footwork2Framework and discrete motion representations.

Motion evaluation

Reference-based measures that separate where a motion goes wrong in space from when it goes wrong in time — signals that DTW-style metrics conflate. See Transition Correctness.

Multimodal & low-resource learning

Aligning audio and motion, and learning structured categories from little labelled data — from South Asian rhythm to ASR lattice rescoring.

Publications & Preprints

Bold indicates my name. under review marks work not yet accepted. A full list is on my Google Scholar profile.

  1. Transition
    Correctness

    Transition Correctness: A Reference-Based Measure for Path and Timing Fidelity in Fine-Grained Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    under review ICLR 2027

    A three-stage, reference-based measure that scores motion transitions in a unified joint-angle space — an anatomical-feasibility gate, path correctness against an expert reference corridor, and timing correctness that separates spatial from pacing error. Needs only 2–3 expert demonstrations and no error-labelled training data; validated on dance, surgical kinematics, and sign language.

    paper (soon)

  2. Investigating Fine-Grained Structure in Discrete 3D Human Motion Representations

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    under review 3DV 2027

    Trains a VQ-VAE on 3D body and articulated-hand motion with no category or performer labels, then probes what the codebook learns. Body tokens recover movement category well above a majority baseline; hand-only tokens carry more performer-specific information — evidence that reconstruction-only objectives implicitly encode category-relevant structure.

    paper (soon)

  3. Footwork2Framework: A Large-Scale Dataset and Generative Framework for Expressive, Fine-Grained Audio-Driven 3D Human Motion

    Ankitha Sudarshan*, Lipisha Nitin Chaudhary*, Tanvi Ranga, Ifeoma Nwogu, Rohini Srihari

    under review WACV 2027

    The first 3D motion dataset with synchronized full-body and hand mesh annotations (1,910 clips, ~47,750 s — 2.5× larger than AIST++), plus a VQ-VAE generation framework conditioned on motion and audio with bidirectional audio–motion alignment and appearance retargeting for avatar synthesis.

    project page paper (soon)

  4. Rhythm
    Learning

    Low-Resource Rhythm Learning of South Asian Beat Structures

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    AAAI EAAI 2026 (PMLR, Vol. 303) · NeurIPS 2025 WiML Workshop

    The first annotated structured-beat dataset for South Asian music (31,560 s, 7 classes). A hybrid MFCC + CLAP model reaches 76.2% accuracy (F1 0.73) over a 69.8% baseline, with zero-shot transfer to rare classes under 30:1 class imbalance.

    paper (soon)

  5. ASR
    Rescoring

    Improving Contextual Recognition in ASR via Semantic Lattice Rescoring

    Ankitha Sudarshan, et al.

    arXiv, 2023 · Purdue University

    A BERT-based NLU rescoring step for ASR lattice decoding, reducing word error rate by 1.36% over Whisper on LibriSpeech. I led a five-person international team from problem formulation through to the write-up.

    arXiv (add link)

    System and Method for Image Analysis and Summarization

    Raksha H, Ankitha Sudarshan, Namitha G  ·  mentor: Dr. Sejal S. Nimbhorkar

    Patent, 2020 · Emotion recognition & video summarization from CCTV surveillance · Funded under NewGen-IEDC (Dept. of Science & Technology, Govt. of India) at B. N. M. Institute of Technology

    An integrated four-module system — recognition, detection, alerting, and summarization — built with OpenPose, Viola–Jones, and clustering, that cuts the time to summarize a surveillance video to roughly one-fifth of the original.
